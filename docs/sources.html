The lookup box above the term field and the English and Dictionary form columns of the word views come from English Wiktionary and its contributors, via Kaikki / Wiktextract (dictionary published 30 October 2026). Dictionary content is shared under CC BY-SA 3.0 / GFDL; the site's MIT code licence does not replace those terms. The reduced extract this site uses, and its checksum, are kept in the GitHub repository.

This is a modern dictionary, not a contextual translation or a scholarly analysis of Kafka's text. A dictionary cannot tell which word a spelling is in a given sentence, so each spelling is filed under its likeliest headword, and the lookup reports how many occurrences are in spellings other words share. Treat those counts as estimates. Kafka's editions use the spelling before the 1996 reform: Schloß, daß, muß are looked up as Schloss, dass, muss, so the castle and the verb schließen ("closed") compete for schloß and the castle, far more frequent here, wins. About 3% of the running text has no dictionary entry, mostly character and place names.

Glosses describe the dictionary word, not the inflected form, and neither glosses nor word-by-word phrase glosses are translations. They are display-only: search terms, counts, and metrics always refer to the German words as written.
